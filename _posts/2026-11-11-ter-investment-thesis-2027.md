## Investment thesis

*Views as of 11 November 2026.*

My investment thesis for TER in 2027 centres on demand for semiconductor testing, particularly wafer and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) testing. I am interested in whether rising demand in these areas could translate into stronger business performance and an attractive investment opportunity.

Teradyne's [2025 annual report](https://investors.teradyne.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001193125-26-059002/ter-20251231.htm) describes share gains in HBM and DRAM final testing. For me, the central question is how much of this opportunity can develop into sustained earnings growth.

## Cyclicality and entry point

I still view Teradyne's earnings as cyclical, so I do not expect growth to follow a smooth path. I regard the sell-off during October and early November 2026 as a potential opportunity to begin building a position gradually, provided the longer-term thesis remains intact. The [share-price history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ter/history/) also shows a subsequent rebound, so this is a dated view of the opportunity rather than a claim that the entry price will remain unchanged.

## Relative valuation and the AMD analogy

Relative valuation is another part of the appeal. In the Stock Analysis snapshots reviewed on 11 November 2026, [TER](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ter/statistics/) traded at approximately 31 times trailing earnings, compared with approximately 40 times for [Advantest Corporation](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tyo/6857/statistics/). However, their reported forward multiples were much closer, at roughly 25 times each. The discount therefore applies to trailing P/E in this comparison; it does not establish that TER is cheaper on every earnings measure. I would assess it alongside the earnings cycle, growth expectations and accounting differences.

To me, the setup has parallels with AMD roughly a year earlier: uncertainty about future orders can coexist with substantial longer-term opportunities. I see a similar question for TER: how much uncertainty around future orders is already reflected in the price? This is an analogy that informs my thinking, rather than a forecast that the two stocks will follow the same path.

## Robotics as an additional growth opportunity

I also see robotics as a potentially underappreciated source of long-term upside. In this analogy, robotics plays a role similar to the CPU opportunity at AMD: an additional growth driver whose eventual contribution may not be fully reflected in investors' expectations.

Teradyne reported that robotics achieved profitability in [Q3 2026](https://investors.teradyne.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001193125-26-434981/ter-ex99_1.htm). My thesis is that the market may still underestimate its longer-term contribution, rather than that robotics has no value assigned to it at all. Sustained growth and profitability would strengthen that view.

## Questions to follow

- How directly could increased wafer and HBM testing demand benefit TER?
- What would demonstrate that this demand is translating into sustainable earnings?
- Does the apparent valuation discount persist when comparing forward or normalised earnings?
- Can robotics sustain profitability and become a more meaningful contributor?
- How much of the semiconductor testing and robotics opportunity is already reflected in the share price?
- What evidence would weaken either part of the thesis?
